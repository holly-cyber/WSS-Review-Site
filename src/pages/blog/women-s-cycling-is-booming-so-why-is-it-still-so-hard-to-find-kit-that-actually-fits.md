## The Gap Between What Women Ride and What Women Can Buy

Women's cycling has never been more visible. Races like the Tour of Britain Women are growing in prestige and public attention, with teams like SD Worx-Protime and Canyon//SRAM fielding world-class rosters that deserve the same spotlight as any men's peloton. The talent is there. The ambition is there. The audiences are there.

What hasn't kept pace is the retail landscape. Walk into most high street sports shops and you'll find rows of men's cycling jerseys, bib shorts designed around male anatomy, and helmets in largely masculine colourways. Women's cycling kit, when it does exist, is often simply a shrunken, pinked-up version of the men's range — or it's priced at a premium for far less choice.

This is a problem that goes beyond aesthetics. Ill-fitting bib shorts lead to saddle sores and chafing. Jerseys that sit wrong affect aerodynamics and comfort on longer rides. Gloves, shoes, and helmets built for larger hands and heads mean women are quite literally riding in gear that puts them at a disadvantage.

## Why Fit Matters More Than You Think

If you've ever returned from a long ride with raw skin or an aching back despite a decent saddle, there's a real chance your kit was the culprit, not your bike setup. Women's bodies carry weight differently, have different hip-to-waist ratios, and typically have a shorter torso relative to overall height — all of which matters enormously when you're in the drops for three hours.

Chamois padding placement in bib shorts is a perfect example. Women's sit bones are generally set wider apart than men's, which means a chamois designed for a male rider simply won't provide cushioning where you actually need it. The same logic applies to jersey cut, arm warmers, and even the placement of pockets.

Getting this right isn't a luxury — it's what keeps you comfortable enough to ride longer, more often, and with more joy. If you're dealing with persistent discomfort on the bike, it's genuinely worth auditing your kit before you assume it's a fit or flexibility issue.
