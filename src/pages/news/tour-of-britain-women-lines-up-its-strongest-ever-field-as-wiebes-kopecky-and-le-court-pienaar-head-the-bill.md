The 2026 Lloyds Tour of Britain Women is shaping up to be one of the most compelling editions of the race yet, with a field that reads like a who's who of contemporary women's cycling. According to Bike Radar, the event — which kicks off this Wednesday — has drawn 13 WorldTour teams alongside five domestic squads and Norwegian Continental outfit Hitec Products–Fluid Control, creating a genuinely international starting grid.

## A Race That Has Grown Into Something Special

This year's edition marks a meaningful step forward in ambition: the event grows to five stages, up from four, and the routing reflects a genuine appetite for challenge. Bike Radar reports that stage three finishes atop the Great Orme, a climb that will suit the punchy climbers in the bunch, while stage four carves through central Wales with over 2,200 metres of climbing packed into 138 kilometres — the kind of terrain that will crack any rider not at their absolute best.

For those of us watching women's cycling develop over the past decade, this matters. Harder courses, more stages, bigger fields — these are the markers of a race that organisers and sponsors genuinely believe in. The Lloyds title sponsorship signals commercial confidence, and that confidence is clearly attracting the sport's biggest names.

## The Names That Will Shape the Race

SD Worx-Protime arrive with arguably the most formidable one-two in women's cycling right now. Lotte Kopecky, winner of this event in 2024, lines up alongside Lorena Wiebes, who according to Bike Radar claimed the green jersey at this year's Tour de France Femmes — a sprint pedigree that will make any bunch finish extremely difficult for rivals to contest. How the team balances those two interests across five varied stages is one of the tactical puzzles worth watching.

Defending champion Ally Wollaston of FDJ–Suez returns to protect her title, bringing with her the confidence of a rider who knows exactly what this race demands. Meanwhile, AG Insurance–Soudal's Kim Le Court Pienaar, a stage winner at this year's Tour de France Femmes, adds further depth to what is already a formidable front group of favourites.

Cycling fans will also enjoy watching the Bäckstedt sisters compete on opposing sides: Zoe lines out for Canyon//SRAM while Elynor represents UAE Team l'Imad. Sibling rivalries have a way of producing memorable moments.

## Why This Field Matters for Women's Sport

From a WSS perspective, the calibre of this start list is worth pausing to appreciate. A domestic British stage race attracting the reigning Tour de France Femmes sprint jersey winner, multiple classics champions and emerging talents from across Europe and beyond is not something to take for granted. It reflects years of steady investment in the women's race calendar and a recognition that audiences — particularly active women who understand the physical demands of endurance sport — are hungry for elite competition on home roads.

The varied stages also mean this is unlikely to be a race decided by a single specialist. Sprinters, climbers and puncheurs all have opportunities, which makes it richer viewing and a truer test of all-round ability. Keep an eye on the Welsh mountains on stage four — that is almost certainly where the race will be won or lost.
